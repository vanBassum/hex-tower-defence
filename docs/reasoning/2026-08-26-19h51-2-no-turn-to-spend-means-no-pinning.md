**Before:** pinning was the obvious way to make a fight a fight — units that
engage become locked together until something resolves it. It is what tactics
games do, and it is what stops a fight from being a thing you stroll out of.

**What changed it:** pinning is a rule about turns. A turn-based game can hold a
unit still because the hold expires when the turn does; the expiry is what makes
it a cost rather than a sentence. There is no turn here to expire, so "held in
place" means held in place forever.

**Now:** nothing pins anybody. A fight is a fact about where things are
standing — any two opposing units on adjacent hexes take casualties off each
other at their own rates, and either may walk away. Combat became a
consequence of position rather than a state that units enter and must be
released from, which also means flanking is not implemented and happens anyway,
falling out of adjacency instead of needing a rule of its own.

A related constraint surfaced in the same pass and is about geography, not
combat: enemies return to the hex the level stood them on when nothing is in
range. Without that post, a player who pokes and retreats drags the picket
across the island, and "don't get close" stops being a decision you can make
twice.

Rests on: movement being continuous rather than turn-gated. Still open — and
material — that the concept doc describes the game as turn-based while this
reasoning assumes it is not.
