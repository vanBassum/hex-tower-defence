title: Failure is the progression mechanic, not the lose condition

**Before:** a level was a thing you won or lost. The old plan's Phase 1 ended
with "add lives/base health and a lose condition" and "a simple win condition
after the final wave", which carries a hidden requirement: every map has to be
balanced so that the player's current force can clear it. A cleared map is then
finished, and a finished map is disposable.

**What changed it:** moving progression onto persistent unlocks. If what the
player finds in a run survives the run, then a run that ends in death has still
advanced them — and once that holds, a map no longer has to be beatable the
first time it is entered.

**Now:** difficulty beyond the player's current means is a feature rather than a
balancing failure. A map can deliberately hold an enemy force the current army
cannot beat, with unexplored ground behind it, and the correct outcome of the
first visit is losing. This removes the entire class of "balance each map
against the current army" problems, and it keeps old maps relevant instead of
turning them into completed levels. The card collection becomes the progression
system, and maps become persistent tactical spaces that open up as it grows.
The intentional first death is a teaching device: the player learns that
discoveries persist by losing and finding they kept something.

Rests on: discoveries persisting across runs, and the player reading their first
death as information rather than punishment. Still open: whether anything
besides cards persists, and what (if anything) a run can permanently cost.

**Follows:** tower defence was dropped rather than extended — towers, waves,
currency and lives deleted, replaced with a Scout, cards, pickups and
exploration.
